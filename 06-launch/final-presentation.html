Slide 05 · Strategy

The Problem Is Already Doing
Our Competitors' Sales Pitch

Strategic Crisis

If RouteLogic does nothing for 12 months there is a real threat the company will lose its standing in the market to leaner, more agile competitors. The platform's complexity is actively driving users to build workarounds — spreadsheets, WhatsApp groups, phone calls, even AI tools — instead of using the product they're paying for.

Users have expressed willingness to look at competitors. The moment of misery: a 3.4× increase in daily time lost to workarounds, from ~9 min to 31 min over two years.

Value Proposition

Simplicity at the core.

For experienced drivers at regional delivery fleet companies, we will prioritise simplicity in core features to eliminate user workarounds and reduce the desire to evaluate competitor products.

Data-Backed Hypothesis
Based on avg. daily time lost to workarounds increasing 3.4× (from ~9 min to 31 min over two years), solving compliance check friction for drivers will reduce daily workaround time, measured by a reduction in compliance check time back toward the ~9-min industry benchmark. Guardrail: Live Dispatch Board and Route Optimizer daily usage must not decline. Go/no-go decision after two weeks of post-launch data.

Competitive Analysis · Workaround Evidence

The product demonstrates strong strategic value at the reporting and operational management layer — but is experiencing critical erosion of trust at the frontline driver level due to compounding stability, latency, and usability failures.

Core workflows are sufficiently broken that users have built parallel systems to compensate:

WhatsApp groups Paper manifests Dispatcher phone calls Screenshots Tribal knowledge AI tools

Without urgent intervention on reliability and workflow simplicity, the platform risks losing renewals despite differentiated administrative capabilities.

Slide 08 · Validation

The Experiment

Hypothesis

One-Click Compliance Checklist for the Experienced Driver will reduce compliance check time-on-task from 14.6 min to ≤ 9 min, as measured over 3 weeks at p<0.05.

Parameters

Baseline14.6 min
MDE−5.6 min
Sample / arm24
Split50 / 50
Duration3 weeks
Significancep < 0.05

Guardrail

Daily active sessions on the Live Dispatch Board and Route Optimizer monitored per arm weekly.

If variant arm shows a statistically significant decrease vs. control at any weekly check-in → pause and rollback, regardless of primary metric.

Hard boundary: Dispatch / Route Optimizer usage must not fall >5% · Workflow completion must not drop below 48%

Control vs. Variant

CONTROL (A)

Current experience: overly complex, compounding tool performance failures. Drivers resort to workarounds to mark stops delivered.

VARIANT (B)

Bundled: (1) auto-population of session fields + (2) single-action submission. Auto-population expected to drive larger time reduction. Follow-on test will isolate if bundle wins.

Ship ✓
Time-on-task ≤ 9 min AND improvement ≥ 5.6 min at p<0.05 AND guardrail not breached after 3 weeks.
Iterate →
Direction is positive but lift is below the MDE. One extension week permitted, then re-evaluate.
Kill ✕
Primary metric shows no improvement or moves negatively. Read date fixed — no early peeking.

Slide 10 · Story

What I Learned Building This

Friction Points

The biggest strategic challenge was sifting through all the data and deciding which problem to solve. Even once chosen, staying narrow in scope — solving just that problem without bleeding into others — was critical.

Keeping the persona as the anchor for every decision had to stay in the forefront throughout. The prototype revealed a gap the PRD missed entirely: what happens when a checklist is partially completed and the dispatch board status still reads "Pending." The PRD also hadn't defined what constitutes the checklist — which items auto-default vs. which require driver confirmation every time.

💡
The Aha Moment

The power of defining success metrics and guardrails before testing or launch. There is so much more than "it worked or it didn't." Making adoption and value creation the heart of any feature launch assessment is what separates good product instinct from rigorous product management.

Key Takeaways · What I Would Do Next
MoSCoW requirements discipline: simple to apply, powerful for protecting scope against good-idea drift
AI as a neutral reviewer: surfaced holes in logic and sharpened specificity I thought was already clear
Journey mapping and roadmap visualisation gave the team a shared language for the problem
GTM planning is often undervalued — this course changed how I think about the full launch arc
Next: bring the experiment brief, shipping criteria, and guardrail framework back to my existing PM team
End-to-end viewpoint: evidence-backed decisions, requirement clarity, and launch planning as a connected system — not separate deliverables
